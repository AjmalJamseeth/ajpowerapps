2 · Cable Selection Advisor

Insulation, armour, screen & sheath recommendation — IEC 60502-1/2 guidance

1 Application & Installation

iDetermines insulation thickness class and which IEC 60502 part applies.
  • LV (up to 1 kV) — IEC 60502-1.
  • MV (1–36 kV) — IEC 60502-2, e.g. 3.6/6 kV, 6/10 kV, 12/20 kV, 18/30 kV classes.
iMatters most for armour choice on single-core circuits: a magnetic (steel) armour on a single-core AC cable sits in an alternating magnetic field and heats up from induced eddy currents — aluminium wire armour (AWA) is used instead when armour is needed on single-core runs. Multi-core cables don't have this issue since the three phases' fields largely cancel inside one sheath.
iMain driver for armour and mechanical protection needs.
  • Direct buried — needs armour for mechanical/rodent protection.
  • Duct/pipe — armour optional, cable is already protected by the duct.
  • Cable tray/ladder (indoor) — armour usually optional unless exposed to impact.
  • Submerged/marine — needs special sheath and often lead or extra-heavy armour.
iNon-linear or high-harmonic loads change neutral sizing and can call for a screen sized for skin-effect heating.
  • General / linear load — standard sizing rules apply.
  • VFD / motor drive — consider screened, symmetrical construction cable.
  • Furnace / arc load — very high harmonic content, oversized neutral typically needed.
iIf the cable runs through an escape route, plant room with high fire load, or safety-critical circuit, low-smoke/fire-retardant or fire-survival cable becomes a requirement rather than an option.

2 Site Conditions

iOil, solvent or chemical exposure (tank farms, process areas) degrades standard PVC oversheath — a chemical-resistant oversheath compound is then needed.
iFrequent excavation nearby, vehicle traffic over shallow burial, or rodent activity increases the case for steel wire armour even where the base environment wouldn't otherwise require it.
iSaline soil, coastal sites, or aggressive industrial atmospheres accelerate armour corrosion — galvanized or extra-corrosion-protected armour, or non-metallic armour, may be preferred.

3 MV System & Earth-Fault Category

iNominal phase-to-phase voltage of the MV system. Used with the earth-fault category to select the cable's rated voltage grade U0/U (Um) from the IEC 60502-2 range (3.6/6 to 18/30 kV).
iOn a solidly-earthed system, an earth fault is cleared quickly and the healthy phases see only a small voltage rise. On an unearthed or resistance-earthed system, an earth fault can persist for some time with the healthy phases rising toward full line-to-line voltage (IEC 60183 "Category C" service) — the cable's insulation voltage grade must be stepped up to suit, not just matched to the nominal phase-to-earth voltage.

4 Hazardous Area Classification

iPer IEC 60079-14. Gas atmospheres use Zone 0/1/2 (0 = continuous, 2 = unlikely/short duration); dust atmospheres use the equivalent Zone 20/21/22. Any classified zone brings mechanical protection, flame-retardance, gland and jointing requirements on top of whatever the base environment/voltage already call for.
iIS circuits use a blue cable sheath and must be routed segregated from non-IS cabling for their entire length to preserve the safety barrier.

6 · Sheath Bonding & Losses

Single-core cable sheath circulating current / induced voltage — IEC 60287-1-1, Cigré guidance

1 Cable & Route Data

iThis module calculates sheath/screen induced voltage from mutual coupling between separate single-core cables. A three-core cable's screen is the return path within the same cable and is not induced into by the other phases in the way a separate single-core screen is — bonding/circulating-current calculations for three-core cable follow a different method and are not covered here. (Source: a published MV cable induced-voltage reference table, which explicitly marks three-core sizes "Not applicable".)
iHow the metallic sheath/screen is earthed along the route.
  • Solid bonding (both ends) — simplest, but a circulating current flows in the sheath for the life of the circuit, causing continuous sheath loss.
  • Single-point bonding — no circulating current, but a standing induced voltage appears at the unearthed end, needing a sheath voltage limiter (SVL).
  • Cross-bonding — sheaths transposed in minor sections so induced voltages largely cancel over a major section; used on longer/higher-current single-core circuits.
iPhysical layout of the three single-core cables.
  • Trefoil (bound/touching) — cables bundled together; lower spacing, lower induced voltage.
  • Flat, spaced — cables laid flat with a gap; larger spacing increases mutual inductance and induced voltage.
iDistance between adjacent cable centres. For trefoil, this is approximately the cable's own overall diameter; for flat formation, the actual laid spacing.
iMean diameter of the metallic sheath or screen layer — from the cable manufacturer's drawing/data sheet.
iDC resistance of the sheath/screen layer per km, temperature-corrected to its expected operating temperature — from the cable data sheet.
iThe load current in the phase conductor that induces the sheath voltage/circulating current.
iFor single-point bonding, the full route length (induced voltage appears at the far end). For cross-bonding, the length of one minor section (route length ÷ 3).

2 Safety Limit

iTypical guidance: 65 V for sheaths accessible to the general public without additional protection (touch voltage limit), higher (commonly up to a few hundred V) where the joint bay/link box is enclosed, locked, and only accessible to authorized/trained personnel with an SVL fitted. Confirm your project's actual limit.
